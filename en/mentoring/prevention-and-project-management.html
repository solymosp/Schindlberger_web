Mentoring

The goal of mentoring

The goal of mentoring is professional development through personal contact. It can be set up for individuals or groups: we mentor managers, project teams, problem-solving teams, internal auditors, customer contacts, and partner departments operating outside quality management in the strict sense. The three areas in which we are most involved:

1

We mentor managers and teams on the effective auditing and operation of automotive (IATF 16949:2016) and general (ISO 9001:2015) quality management systems, primarily to optimise the resources needed to run the system and to close audit nonconformities efficiently and economically.

2

We mentor automotive project teams and project organisations to put project management techniques into practice and develop project competence. On request, we also mentor production units on the requirements of project management, so that they can act as competent partners in working with project organisations, even when the organisation responsible for the project is geographically separated from the manufacturing site.

3

We teach and transfer automotive methodologies to organisations that can apply them to significant advantage in their own industry, or that are preparing to enter the automotive supply chain and want to learn the relevant requirements, master the necessary methodologies step by step, and build up their development-related resource needs in a planned way while keeping them to the minimum possible.

Workshops

For mentoring groups, we organise professional skills development workshops. In these workshops we work on live topics, where the goal – beyond working through the task at hand – is to develop the group's practical implementation competence. In the automotive industry, we currently see the most important competences to develop in the following areas:

‍System competence:
Interpreting, mapping, auditing and operating the "quality" system. Effective management of documentation systems, building auditor capabilities, and raising the quality capability of partner departments to the appropriate level.

Project management competence:
The organisational set-up of effective project management, capacity planning, embedding the necessary quality knowledge, project handover and takeover, ramp-up management. Effectively feeding knowledge gained in one project back into other projects, and developing the ability of production sites to make their voice heard with development, where the two are geographically separated.

Competence in problem-solving and troubleshooting processes:
Resource planning, proper implementation of the risk-based approach, consideration of second- and third-order effects, cost awareness, sustaining the improvements achieved, and eliminating recurring nonconformities wherever possible.

Human resources competence:
Developing an approach based on human knowledge and expertise, competence development, effective onboarding systems, in-house training of specialists, and managing the risk of knowledge loss.
